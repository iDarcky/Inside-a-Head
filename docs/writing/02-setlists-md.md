# Setlists.md — in your own words

An offline chord-chart app for worship teams. Live at setlistsmd.insideahead.com, used by one church's team, two more interested, beta planned for 1 February 2027.

## The spark

1. What's your own role in a worship team? Describe a Sunday morning before Setlists.md existed: the PDFs, the screenshots, the moment the Wi-Fi died.
2. There's an older private repo, `worship-setlists`. Was that the first attempt? What happened to it and what did you take from it?
3. Why build it yourself instead of using an existing app (OnSong, Planning Center, SongSelect…)?

## Discovery

4. Who did you talk to? Your own band, other leaders, guest musicians? What did they complain about?
5. The persona is "a worship leader with a guitar and a tablet, leading 3–7 people, no Ableton rig". How did you arrive at that? Who is it based on?
6. What surprised you while researching how teams actually prepare and play?

## Definition

7. Offline-first was a hard rule. What made it non-negotiable?
8. Songs as plain Markdown files: why that format, and was anyone sceptical?
9. You walked ten flows from "first launch" to "Sunday morning". How did that review work, and which decision did it change most?
10. "A church is one space, a setlist is the service." What were the alternatives you rejected?
11. What's deliberately out of scope?

## Design

12. The design system is called Lydian, after the musical mode. Why that name, and what does it mean for how the app looks and feels?
13. What's it like designing for a tablet on a mic stand, mid-song, sometimes in bad light? Any details that only came from using it live?
14. A feature you redesigned several times, and why.

## Build

15. How is it built, in your words? What part of it are you proudest of?
16. What broke in real use (at church) that never broke while testing?
17. How do you work with AI on this one? Different from The Retro Circuit?

## Launch and results

18. How did the first church start using it? Who convinced whom?
19. What do they use most, and what do they ignore?
20. A moment during a service when it saved the day (or didn't).
21. The plan: soft launch with 5–10 teams in December, public beta 1 February. How will you find those teams, and what will "success" mean?

## Lessons and next

22. What has this project taught you about building for a small, specific audience?
23. If it grows, does it stay free? Any thoughts on a business model?
24. What's the one feature you're most excited to build next?
